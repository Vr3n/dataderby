Act one · the league  ·  home advantage

Home advantage fell off a cliff,
then remembered itself. Mostly.

Home sides took 64.7 percent of points in 2009/10. A decade of slow erosion, then 2020/21 at 48.7: home teams took fewer points than visitors for the only time on record.

The control group

No annotation table needed

The dip lines up with the behind-closed-doors season exactly. The warehouse never heard of COVID; the shape of the line knows anyway.

One query

Home points over total points per season, from the overall/home/away splits nobody had to pivot.

Act two · the game  ·  goals

A decade of 1.4.
Then 2023/24 happened.

Team goals per game sat between 1.28 and 1.43 for fourteen seasons. Then 2023/24 scored 3.28 per match, the highest in the dataset by distance, and the line came straight back down.

City 96, and still second-best story

Attack peaked with the scoring wave

Manchester City's 96 in 2023/24 is the third-highest team total ever recorded here, behind only the 102 of 2019/20 and the 99 of 2021/22. Same club, all three.

One query

Total goals over total played per season. The warehouse never stores a league total; every total is derived the same way, every time.

Per-team goals per game; double it for per-match. City 102 (19/20) remains the highest team total.

Why it is one query

Same grain, one join

Performance facts and table facts share (snapshot, team). Best rating versus champion is a join across that key, not a spreadsheet merge.

The caveat

Ratings describe how a team played; points describe what it won. The schema keeps both and refuses to confuse them.

Eight findings, zero blocks

Every row is stored exactly as reported and flagged by the audit. The old rule blocked any total below the maximum; Southampton proved that wrong, and the rule now blocks only the impossible.

The design point

Points are a reported measure, never derived. A deduction is data, and the audit carries the arithmetic beside it instead of rewriting history.

Act three · the tool  ·  eleven leagues, one contract

Same columns.
Eleven leagues. One query.

* UCL: 36-team format, small sample. A question, not a ranking.

The spread is narrow: 1.28 to 1.62 across ten leagues, with one outlier. That compression is the story. Eleven competitions feed one contract, so comparing them is a GROUP BY, not a project.

Why it works

The columns never change

Sixty performance measures and thirty table measures, same names, same order, tested against the database. A new league is rows, not a rewrite.

Caveat, stated

Ratings compress too (6.62 to 6.76 everywhere). Either every league defends the same, or the rating scale does. I would not bet either way on this sample.

Act three · the tool  ·  the verdict

The schema held.
Here is the receipt.

Mechanism 1

One grain, one query

One team in one snapshot, everywhere. Nine analyst questions, nine queries, zero staging tables, zero merges.

Mechanism 2

One clock, honest history

observed_at orders everything; accepted_at audits it. Backfills slot into history without rewriting it.

Mechanism 3

Gaps stay visible

A missing measure is NULL. A deduction is stored with its delta. A missing season is a documented row, not a silent hole.

The test

Nothing in this deck required a second source, a notebook join, or trusting the loader. That is the whole product.

Three mechanisms, already named on page two: what, when, and whether it is the same observation.

Act three · the tool  ·  the friction log

Three things
that bit me.

Bite 1 · the narrow view

History hides the columns

v_standings_history carries rank, points and form only. I needed goal difference, so I dropped one level to the fact table. Lesson: views are shortcuts, facts are the contract.

Bite 2 · the unit trap

1.39 is not 2.78

Goals per team per game versus per match differ by exactly double. The grain makes the answer right; only the analyst's label can make it wrong.

Bite 3 · the playoff trap

The selected stage lied

Four seasons resolved to playoff stages, not leagues. From then on I enumerated the dropdown instead of trusting the default. Verify the grain before you query it.

Friction is evidence too. Each bite changed a habit, a paragraph, or a script, and each one is now written down somewhere.

The rule, kept

An addition needs a question that cannot be answered without it. Until then, the smaller warehouse is the more honest one.

None of these change what the warehouse promises. They change what it can be asked.
